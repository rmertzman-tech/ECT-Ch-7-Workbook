0
Instructor opening · 4–6 min
ORIENT — Chapter 7 after the reading

What changes when we can compute more of the consequences?

You already know the simple Chapter 7 task: Consequentialism asks what follows from an action; Care ethics asks what responsibilities arise within concrete relationships and dependencies. Today we add a complication: modern computational tools can show us more possible futures and more kinds of affected people than unaided intuition can hold at once.

Doing language: a person is not a number waiting to receive an outcome. Each person is in an unfolding Doing : assembled from a history, acting with present capacities and constraints, and facing a field of possible next steps. A consequence matters partly because it can open, narrow, redirect, or damage those future arcs.
Today's critical-thinking question: computation can expand a map. It does not automatically tell us what to recommend or who is authorized to decide. Forecast ≠ Recommendation ≠ Authorization.
Instructor move: Ask: “If a computer could predict every consequence perfectly, would that settle the ethical decision?” Take 2–3 responses without resolving them yet.
1
Individual first · 5 min
ENTER — The Storm Decision

Preserve your first map before the models get better

A community college must decide by 6:00 p.m. whether to change tomorrow's operations because a severe storm may affect the region. Closing too early can disrupt classes, labs, work schedules, childcare, and pay. Staying fully open can expose students and employees to unsafe travel if conditions worsen.

The administration initially receives one headline forecast: “There is a 40% chance of serious local impacts tomorrow.”

For now, assume you know nothing else.

Talk / countermap · 7 min
BUILD THE TASK — Before optimizing

What are we actually trying to do?

EHI task-building rule: a model can optimize the wrong task extremely well. Before asking “What should the college do?” define the task precisely enough that success and failure can be inspected.
Mapper
State the task and what counts as success.
Challenger
Find what the task definition leaves outside.
License-keeper
Flag any conclusion that outruns the evidence.

Whole-class harvest: Put 3–4 task definitions on the board. Ask which one a powerful forecasting system should optimize. Do not choose a winner yet.

PALMER — Computation expands uncertainty

One forecast is not the same thing as the shape of the possibilities

Tim Palmer's ensemble approach is useful here because it replaces one confident-looking trajectory with many model runs that begin from slightly different plausible conditions. Modern computation makes it practical to inspect a distribution of possible futures rather than hide uncertainty inside one headline number.

Illustrative classroom ensemble — not a real weather forecast. Ten simplified runs of tomorrow's local impact:

4 low-impact runs · 3 moderate · 3 high-impact. The point is not that this distribution is “the answer.” The point is that the map now contains multiple plausible trajectories and their spread.

Consequentialist enhancement: computation can widen the consequence map from “what I expect” to a structured range of plausible outcomes. But expected outcomes remain sensitive to what we count, how we model, and which consequences the task definition includes.
4
Instructor unlock 2 · 10 min
FARMER — Computation expands the people in the map

The average student is not any actual student

Doyne Farmer's work is a useful comparator because computational models can represent heterogeneous agents rather than compress a population into one “representative” person. That lets us ask not only “What is the average consequence?” but “How do different situated agents carry different consequences?”

Situated Doing If campus stays open If campus closes / shifts remote
Janelle — works hourly on campus and needs the paycheck Income preserved; travel risk may rise Safer travel; possible lost pay
Mateo — uses paratransit and has limited route flexibility Transportation may become unreliable before general travel looks dangerous Remote access may preserve participation if technology works
Rina — student parent using campus childcare Childcare remains available; travel risk applies to child too Childcare disappears; may lose work or class time
Devon — has a required lab that is difficult to reproduce remotely Academic progress preserved Lab delay may create downstream scheduling costs
Professor Lee — commuting from a flood-prone area May face risk different from campus conditions Needs a workable way to teach and support students remotely
Consequentialist enhancement: better computation can expose distribution, variance, and burden export—not just totals. A policy with a good average can still reshape particular people's Doings very differently.
5
Conversation · 8 min
CARE — What does the consequence map still not contain?

A relationship is not merely another variable

Care ethics does not simply add “one more consequence” to the total. It can change the ethical question by making dependency, responsiveness, vulnerability, history, and responsibility between particular people morally salient.

Now add this: the college has repeatedly told students, faculty, and staff that safety decisions will not punish people for circumstances beyond their control. Several instructors also know individual students whose transportation, health, caregiving, or disability situations make a uniform rule especially costly.

Doing interpretation: care asks whether our action keeps another person's Doing intelligible and possible—not whether we can guarantee their preferred outcome, but whether we recognize the dependencies and standing that make their situation more than a row in an optimization table.
6
Whole-class licensing board · 8 min
EHI — Where does the arrow need a bridge?

Forecast ≠ recommendation ≠ authorization

Motivated firewall: a good forecast can support a recommendation, but it cannot by itself supply the values, standing, tradeoffs, or legitimate authority needed to authorize action.
FORECAST
What is likely or possible?
RECOMMEND
What should be done, given specified goods and burdens?
AUTHORIZE
Who may decide or impose the action, under what constraints?
Board format: write student examples as arrows: evidence → claim → recommendation → authorization . Circle any arrow that needs another premise, value, affected-person input, or authority condition.

Fast transfer · 5 min
TRANSFER — The Emergency Grant

Can you carry the architecture without repeating the storm?

A college has a limited emergency-aid fund. A simple model recommends giving smaller grants to more students because that maximizes the number helped. Staff members argue that a few students face eviction or utility shutoff and need much larger grants immediately. The college must decide whether to optimize reach, severity, relational knowledge, or some combination.
