1. Arrive

Consequences matter, but totals can hide people.

In Chapter 7, you learned to ask what an action or policy actually helps produce. This workbook helps you map benefits, harms, affected people, distribution, and repair.

Privacy reminder: You may use the provided case, a fictional example, a public example, a historical example, a composite example, or a low-stakes personal example. Do not disclose private, identifying, or sensitive information.
2. Read the Case

The Strict Attendance Policy

A course adopts a strict attendance policy to improve learning and make expectations fair. Students who miss more than a set number of classes lose major credit, regardless of the reason. The policy does encourage some students to attend more regularly. But it also places heavy pressure on students with caregiving duties, transportation instability, illness, disability-related needs, or unpredictable work schedules. A rule intended to support learning may produce unequal burdens.

3. Intended Goal

Name the action or policy and its intended benefit.

Intention vs consequence: intention is what someone means to do; consequence is what the action or policy actually helps produce.
4. Consequence Map

Trace benefits, harms, and affected people.

5. Distribution

Check who receives the benefit and who carries the burden.

Distribution: how benefits and burdens are spread across different people or groups.
6. Burden Export

Ask whether the success measure hides a cost.

Burden export: when a cost is made invisible or pushed onto people who are not fully counted in the success measure.
7. Responsibility and Limits

Do not let either intention or consequences flatten the people involved.

8. Repair Response

Use feedback from consequences to improve the route.
